# Source caveats

These are caveats on a few of the deck's figures and sources, plus related sources to have ready if students ask. They aren't full slide notes. All quotes, sources, and links in the deck were verified by hand on 2026-10-12.

## Facts to keep in mind

- **Havasupai case:** Arizona State University is a public university governed by the Arizona Board of Regents, so this is a state government case, though not a Californian one.
- **AskCA:** the AskCA policy pages don't name a model provider, though the Governor's release does. The "Vendor transparency" box on the questions slide points at this gap.
- **EDD:**
  - The deck uses the LAO's 2022 figures: 1.1M claims suspended, at least 600K legitimate.
  - The LAO's 2021 handout gave 1.4M, suspended around Jan 1, 2021.
- **CalHHS DDG Edition 2.0 (Oct 2025):**
  - The rule of fewer than 11 applies when the publication score is 13 or higher, with a denominator threshold of 20,000.
  - The slide simplifies this to "can trigger suppression."

## Related sources (not on slides)

- Joerges (1999), "Do Politics Have Artefacts?", disputes Winner's Robert Moses bridges example. It's useful if a student raises it.
- Strategic Growth Council (Feb 2026) draft Tribal Gap Analysis recommends removing state program requirements that undermine tribal data sovereignty.
- CalEPA Tribal Policy (2015) acknowledges confidentiality needs for culturally significant places and resources.
