Research done 2026-10-12. Every claim on the slides comes from a cited source, but the items below need a human check before the lecture.

## Quotes to check against print or the original page

- **Madison, Federalist No. 51:** verified verbatim against the Library of Congress text.
- **Proposition 11 ballot argument (1972):** "The right of privacy is the right to be left alone. It is fundamental and compelling." Verified only as quoted on SCOCAblog, which cites Ballot Arguments p. 27. The original pamphlet PDF (UC Law SF repository, ca_ballot_props/762) blocked automated download. Check the wording and page in the PDF.
- **Tuck (2009):** all three quotes were checked verbatim against the full-text PDF (masslegalservices.org copy of *HER* 79(3)). Page numbers (413, 416) come from the PDF's page markers. The book's site blocked automated access, so confirm the exact wording (with or without a comma) in Chapter 4 online: https://data-feminism.mitpress.mit.edu/pub/h1w0nbqp
- **GIDA CARE one-liners:** taken verbatim from the GIDA one-pager. They use British spellings ("recognised"), which stay as written inside the quotes.

## Facts to keep in mind

- **Tribal data cases (What is Indigenous data sovereignty? slide):**
  - The Census 5.64% figure is verified against the Census Bureau's March 10, 2022 release.
  - The *Cobell* settlement ($3.4B, final approval June 20, 2011) is verified against the Interior Department's release. The slide sticks to what that release says (a class action over trust accounts and land). It doesn't claim the government "failed to account."
  - The Havasupai settlement (2010, about $700K to 41 members, samples returned) is from news coverage plus Mello & Wolf (NEJM, 2010; DOI confirmed via Crossref).
  - Arizona State University is a public university governed by the Arizona Board of Regents, so this is a state-government case, though not a Californian one.

- **AskCA:**
  - The site labels it "Prototype." The Governor's release talks about recruiting beta testers.
  - The 60-day chat retention comes from the privacy policy. The no-training statement comes from the About page, not the privacy policy.
  - The AskCA policy pages don't name a model provider, though the Governor's release does. The "Vendor transparency" box on the questions slide points at this gap.
- **Engaged California:** I couldn't find a stated retention period or any identity verification process. The deck doesn't claim either.
- **EDD:**
  - The deck uses the LAO's 2022 figures: 1.1M claims suspended, at least 600K legitimate.
  - The LAO's 2021 handout gave 1.4M, suspended around Jan 1, 2021.
- **CalHHS DDG Edition 2.0 (Oct 2025):**
  - The rule of fewer than 11 applies when the publication score is 13 or higher, with a denominator threshold of 20,000.
  - The slide simplifies this to "can trigger suppression."
- **CNRA Indigenous data sovereignty toolkit:** the page couldn't be opened directly (403) and was verified only from search indexing. Click through before citing.
- **Truth and Healing Council final report:** I couldn't confirm whether it has been released, so the deck doesn't claim it.
- **leginfo.legislature.ca.gov URLs:** they blocked automated checks. They are well-formed, but click-check them.
